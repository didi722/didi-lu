General rules and League structure

What is the League?

Sant’Alvise Pokémon League is an unofficial Pokémon battle platform originally created by Didi and Lu to have an excuse to stay in contact as long-distance friends while having fun with competitive Pokémon.

The League is an informal organization that hosts Pokémon competitions, usually organized in Seasons. At the end of every season, the player with the largest number of points collected throughout the battles is declared the winner.

Who can participate to the League?

There are no limitations for whom can be part of the league. At the beginning of every season every contender can subscribe his teams in respect of the specific rules for that competition.

It’s highly recommended to inform one of the league's founders of your intention to participate: since the system is not fully automated, there are some structural differences depending on the number of participants that must be organized in advance.

Structure of the League

The League is its own independent organizational entity that hosts Pokémon Tournaments organized as Seasons. A Season is made of the total number of PVPs between each participant following the specific structure previously decided.

The battle organization follows the Set-Match-Showdown system, which is described as follows:

  • A Set is a single battle 4vs4 between two Pokémon trainers. Which one kos the other team first, while having at least one Pokémon surviving, wins the set. Draws are extremely rare and not contemplated in the point system: whenever it happens the Set is considered invalid. Ribbon for draws?
  • An uneven (usually 3 or 5) number of subsequent Sets between the same pair of teams creates a Match. A Match is the minimum unity that enables the point acquisition.
  • An uneven (usually 3) number of Matches between two players following the same Format creates a Showdown. N number of Showdowns create a Season. A Showdown must be completed in its entirety during a single meeting. There is no possibility of dividing it in different days of times.

Battle organization and point system

When, how, and where the battles are hosted

Every battle is hosted on Pokémon Showdown. At the beginning of a Season when the number of participant and Showdowns is officially decided, each player can independently organize its Showdowns with the others (there is not a fixed calendar).

Every battle must be played in person or during a videocall: this facilitates communications between players, to some extent prevents cheating, it’s fun for the reaction and commentary, and it’s a great way to stay in contact. Battles inside a season are mandatory public for the players of that season.

Seasons

What is a Season?

A Season is the name given to the official competition hosted by Sant’Alvise League. A Season is a series of PVP Showdowns – of previously selected Formats – between the N number of trainers who subscribed to the specific competition.

How to participate to a Season

In order to participate to a Season, trainers must submit their subscription during the pre-Season time. Once a Season, with the selected formats and number of matches, is announced, trainers have a determined amount of time (compatibly to the length of the Season and the number of matches, following the general principle of common sense) to prepare their teams and enrol them in the dedicated page. Only the trainers subscribed with their enrolled teams can participate to a Season.

Trainers who want to compete in a Season must enrol at least one (1) team for each Format of that season. There is a limitation to the maximum number of teams that can be enrolled per Format in a specific Season and depends on the number Showdowns-per-Format. The table below serves as a guideline:

The enrolment of a team consists of the registration of the official name of the team and its unique code – generated by the website once it has been previously uploaded to the Trainer’s Box – to the dedicated page.

A team can be enrolled only once within a specific Season. There are no other specific limitations to the enrolment of Teams, except for what concerns coherence with the format and teambuilding rules. The same team can be freely enrolled in different Seasons.

How to create a Season

Only the Founders can create a Season. Everyone can present their proposal for the creation of a Season (with number of matches, formats, official name), which will be evaluated (feasibility, compatibility with the Rulebook) and then approved or rejected. What follow are the general rules for the creation of a season.

  • The length of a season is calculated upon the total number of Showdowns using the following formula:
    Season Length = Formats (F) x Showdowns-per-Format (SpF) x Trainers (T)
    This number represents the “ideal” length of a season. Since we are all adults with jobs (more or less) and responsibilities, the “actual” length of a Season is calculated upon the number of Showdowns a single Trainer has to play. This number is called Commitments-per-Trainer (CpT) and is calculated with the following formula:
    CpT = F x SpF x (T-1)

    From the CpT it is possible to calculate the “temporal” length of a Season, which represents the actual duration (in days) of a season, and sets the beginning and the mandatory ending of that Season.

    Every Showdown must be completed within the duration of the season (see above for info on how to complete your Showdowns). Following the principle of common sense, ten (10) days is the reasonable time interval between Showdowns that a Trainer can sustain without interfering with their personal life.

  • Every time a Season is created the proponents can suggest the numbers of F and SpF, however they must align with the following instructions. The minimum number of CpTs needed to create a Season (mini-Season) is six (6), and the maximum number of CpTs (maxi-Season) is between eighteen (18) and twenty-four (24), compatibly to the principle of common sense (depending on the time of the year and commitment of the Trainers).

  • Whenever the limit of the maxi-Season is exceeded, the Season can be divided into different Divisions (randomly dividing the Trainers into N Divisions (D), depending on the CpS). At the end there will be N different Rankings. The Trainers occupying the 1st position in every division will embark on the Champion Season.

    In the Champion Season the Trainers will challenge each other of each format of the regular Season for a single Showdown of one (1) match, creating a final ranking. The other Trainers will be ranked according to the points achieved during the regular Season.

    The absolute length of season that includes N Divisions is:

    Season Length: (F x SpF x T) + (F x D)
    Note: ideally it is impossible to know in advance the number of Trainers that decide to participate to a season. However, since we are all friends and responsible people, the number can be known in advance with minimal margin of error by asking whoever wants to compete. In general, having an hypothetical number of participants is useful for creating a Season (specifically the number of Formats and SpF).
  • Proponents can freely select the Formats, or create them specifically, for a Season (they must follow the rules for the creation of a Format
  • There are no specific rules for the selection of the name, except the principle of common sense.
  • Every Season must be unanimously validated by the Founders of the League.
  • Proponents are invited to create feasible Seasons, to be reasonable and judicious, to not waste the Founders time and patience. Otherwise, there will be consequences.

Battle formats

What is a format?

A Format consists of the specific combination of guidelines and rules that constitute the general architecture of a Showdown. Those refer to the style of battle, the battle rules, the Pokémon selection, the movepool selection, and the instrument selection.

Trainers can propose the creation of a format to the Founders who, as for the Season creation, ultimately approve or reject the proposal. The following paragraphs deepen the explanation of the general structure of a Format and serve the Trainers as a guideline for the creation of future Formats.

The Ontological Coherence

This is the only universal and mandatory rule within every Format. The Ontological Coherence principle states that a Pokémon cannot learn moves outside its official movepool or have an ability different from the ones specified on its official page on Bulbapedia (link). This principle is applied coherently with the Pokémon Generation applied to a specific Format.

Bulbapedia serves as the only source of information regarding the application of the Ontological Coherence principle.

Format types

Every Format in the Sant’Alvise League falls into three possible types: Anything Goose, Official VGC, and Regulated Format.

  • Anything Goose Format type enables full customization. There are ideally no rules except for the Ontological Coherence principle.
  • Official VGC Format type follows one of the specific regulations of the official Pokémon VGC championships played so far.
  • The Regulated Format type is always subjected and must respect the RF Battle Rules, no matter the specific combination of rules within a Format.

RF battle rules

The General Battle Rules basically reflect the current general rules of the Pokémon VGC Championships. For the Sant’Alvise League those rules are:

  • Sleep clause: you cannot put to sleep both of your opponent’s Pokémon on the field;
  • Move clause 1: you cannot teach the same move to a Pokémon twice in the same moveset;
  • Move clause 2: you cannot teach moves banned by the official Pokémon VGC regulation;
  • Item clause 1: you can use an item only once per team;
  • Item clause 2: you cannot use items banned by the official Pokémon VGC regulation;
  • Pokémon clause 1: you can use a species of Pokémon only once per team;
  • Pokémon clause 2: when available, you can use a maximum of two (2) Uber Pokémon when available;
  • Pokémon clause 3: you cannot use Pokémons banned by the official Pokémon VGC regulation.

Formats, categories and creation

Here below are listed all the possible categories of rules that can be decided when creating a format. Whenever a category is not specified it automatically refers to the regulation of the Pokémon VGC of the Generation selected (if there is no gen selection, refer to the latest one).

Battle style

  • Refers to the specific style of battle to be played during a match. Now there are only four styles of battle: Single, Double, Triple, Rotation. Whenever not specified, the battle style will always be a Double battle.

Gen selection

  • Refers to the limitations to the generation of Pokémon that can be applied. Any possible combination or selection is contemplated. There can be mono-gen formats, only a selection of some gens, or an interval of gens.

    As far as what concerns the Ontological Coherence principle and the other specific selections below, the reference point for the non-specified rules is the VGC regulation of latest gen of the selection (e.g. if you want to play a format with the gen selection 3-7, all the other specific selections depend on the 7th gen regulation)

  • There is another possible variable in gen selection that is represented by the opposition “within/up-to”:
    • Within a gen means that the other specific selections depend on everything that was allowed in that gen by the VGC regulation
    • Up-to a gen means that the other specific selections depend on everything that was available up-to the latest gen selected.

Generational mechanic

  • Can be allowed or not (On/Off). The type of mechanic refers to the ones allowed in (or available up to) the generations selected.

Pokémon selection

  • Pokémon selection is entirely dependent on the gen selection and cannot be incoherence between the two.
  • The selection can be based on the official categories of Pokémon within Showdown (Ubers, OU, UU, etc.). If not specified, refer to the specific gen selected.
  • There can be other forms of restriction to the Pokémon selection. Here there is a list of possible categories of selection:
    • Types (monotypes, bitypes, etc.)
    • BST (limitation to the Base Stat amount. Up to, not less than, between, etc.)
    • Colour (monocolour, bi-colour, rainbow [all different])
    • Evolutionary stage (only first stage, only middle evolutions, etc.)
  • Pokémon repetitions, mono-legendary, mythical Pokémon are allowed only in the Anything Goose Format type

Item selection

  • Item selection is entirely dependent on the gen selection and cannot be incoherence between the two (no 6th gen Pokémon with up to 3rd gen items or 7th gen items)
  • There can be other forms of restriction to the item selection (e.g. only berries, no berries, etc.)
  • If not specified every allowed item in the selected gen is available
  • Mono-items are allowed only in the Anything Goose Format type

    • Move selection

  • Move selection is entirely dependent on the gen selection and cannot be incoherence between the two (Ontological Coherence principle)
  • There can be other forms of restriction to the move selection (e.g. only damage moves, only status moves, etc.)
  • Mono-moves are allowed only in the Anything Goose Format type.

Team building

Team building depends entirely on the characteristics of the Format to battle in. Organizers will specify the best Showdown category that enables Trainers to freely create their teams, validate them and battle. If there are any doubts, issues or limitations, contact the Founders.

Trainer's box

In their personal profile, Trainers have access to their Box of Pokémon where there is all the information of their teams available to be enrolled in a Season. Trainers can upload as many teams as they want following these steps:

  • Click the “Import/Export” button on your team preview on Showdown;
  • Copy all the text;
  • Paste it in the dedicated textbox on the website;
  • Choose the name of your team (there cannot be two or more teams with the same name. Team’s name must be different [note: changing upper and lowercase letters does not count as a new name]);
  • Choose the specific Format in which the team will compete.

The website automatically generates a unique code for every team uploaded. To ensure fairness and prevent cheating, the uploaded teams cannot be modified or delated (if there are some issues or there have been a mistake in uploading a team, please contact the Founders).

All registered teams are available in the section “Box” of the Trainer’s profile. Trainers do not have access to other’s boxes. The Box contains both open-sheet teams and closed-sheet teams with full information.

Similarity restriction

There is only one limitation to the Trainer’s Box, which is the Similarity Restriction. Within a specific Format, Trainers cannot upload new teams too similar to previous ones already registered into their Boxes. Two teams that compete in the same format are considered too similar when they share more than two (2) Pokémon of the same species.

The Similarity Restriction does not apply to Anything Goose and Official VGC formats.

Rules of conduct

There are only a few and simple, but mandatory rules:

  • Every Trainer is committed to pursuing mutual trust, mutual respect, and common sense;
  • None of us is a sbirro of wants to be so. For this reason, the correct functioning of the Sant’Alvise League is entirely grounded in mutual trust, respect and common sense;
  • A Trainer cannot engage in behaviours explicitly aimed to obstacle, interfere, or boycott a season, a showdown, or a match;
  • Physical violence is absolutely prohibited;
  • Trainers must follow the Rulebook and its rules as the only source of information for the Sant’Alvise League. If the Rulebook does not contain the answer to their doubts, or present some issues, the Trainers can appeal to the Founders;
  • The Trainers must respect the decisions of the Founders, who have the last word on major decisions concerning the League.

Rules for editing the Rulebook

The Rulebook is the only official source of information on the Sant’Alvise League and must be trusted and respected by both Trainers and Founders. Everything that is written inside the Rulebook must be coherent and aims to be the fairest and logical possible.

Since there is always something to improve and we are not perfect, the content of the Rulebook can be changed by editing already written rules, by adding new rules, by delating old rules. The Rulebook can be modified only by the Founders and with their unanimous agreement. Trainers can suggest edits to the Founders. The only inalienable condition for every change of the Rulebook is not to break the Rule 0.

Founders' responsibilities

Founders vow to not be dictators or sbirri, to respect Trainers and their proposals, to respect the Rulebook and to monitor its application with fairness and judgement. Founders vow to commit to the good functioning of Sant’Alvise League, solving issues, improving the overall experience and assuring a good entertainment experience.

Founders vow to respect each other as good friend, to respect the principle of unanimity when deciding on the Sant’Alvise League, to always pursue compromise when disagreement occurs.
